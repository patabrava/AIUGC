# Copywriting — customer outcome, credible value, and action

Use for marketing, positioning, offers, acquisition/onboarding messages, and persuasive product copy. Read `bridgecode/writing.md`; load `bridgecode/taste.md` for consequential discretionary choices. Identify the actual audience, customer problem, desired outcome, offer, supporting proof, objections, appropriate action, and claim limits. For unresolved direction, compare perspectives or the smallest useful amalgam through Person, Core, and representative work, explaining distinct responsibilities and practical consequences.

Donald Miller's StoryBrand mechanism places the customer in the central role and the product/brand as a guide: clarify the customer's problem, provide a credible plan, make the next action understandable, and show the supported outcome and real stakes. Alex Hormozi's value mechanism examines desired outcome, perceived likelihood of achievement, time delay, and effort/sacrifice. Use the first to organize customer understanding and the second to clarify value and friction; the evidenced brief resolves conflicts. These are decision tools, not a mandatory page template or permission to imitate a person's voice. Apply Taste to the open expressive choices; user-selected frameworks remain fixed.

Explain what changes for the customer and how the product makes it possible. Use proof actually available; never invent testimonials, performance claims, guarantees, scarcity, urgency, customer counts, or outcomes. Identify provisional copy and missing evidence. Describe speed and ease only to the extent the product supports them. Use recognizable terms, specific verbs, consistent actions, honest availability, and useful error/empty-state recovery. Product interfaces omit irrelevant framework names, build tooling, internal agents, storage details, and development progress; technical audiences receive details when those details help their task. Functional UI controls should communicate their operation rather than carry a sales pitch.

Review whether a stranger in the intended audience can identify the problem, expected benefit, reason to trust it, required effort, and next action without reconstructing internal implementation. Check that every claim and control matches real capability, that hierarchy serves the brief, and that optional rhetoric has not displaced useful information. Stop when the requested messages meet those conditions. Sources for the mechanisms: https://storybrand.com/downloads/your-brand-is-not-the-hero.pdf and https://www.acquisition.com/training/offers4 (The Value Equation).
